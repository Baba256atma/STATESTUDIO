# OVS:AUDIT — Object & Visual System

## Stop condition

Inspect the certified Stage, DTH, DTH-EXP, STAGE-PROD, and SCENE-ORG visual capabilities. Record what the new 3D Object and isometric visualization language can reuse, and what it still lacks. Do not implement OVS:1.

## Verdict

AUDIT COMPLETE. OVS:1 has not started.

The workspace can host a richer object language without a new Stage, Theatre, chart, camera, or region authority. What the manager sees today is a near-frontal perspective stage of camera-facing volumes, plus semantic Theatre roles and 2D contextual charts. An isometric object and data theatre does not exist.

## Current picture

The live Executive Stage camera is a fixed perspective camera: default elevation 18°, azimuth 8°, field of view 42°. STAGE-2D:1 forbids orbit, pan, zoom, and focus-driven camera moves. Objects move; the camera does not. The viewing policy is deliberately near-frontal so the network does not read as recession or diagonal distortion.

STAGE-3DOBJ:1–3 can give those objects restrained depth, bevel, family silhouettes, face symbols, and a form-first material hierarchy. Geometry origin is “back on the plane, front toward the camera,” with `rotationX` and `rotationY` locked at 0. State must not change depth or form. These modules default on, with query and environment overrides, and they have invariant tests. They have no certification artifact, and they are not an isometric language.

DTH-EXP visual roles (`bubble`, `bar`, `flow-node`, `impact-node`, `cause-node`, `risk-marker`, `time-point`, `execution-marker`, `outcome-marker`) are temporary presentation roles. DTH-EXP:3B recipes and DTH-EXP:5A layout place them in normalized stage space (`x`/`y` in `[0,1]`). They do not emit isometric meshes. The NexoTime boundary remains: a timeline is a visualization technique inside NexoTime, not a second Theatre.

STAGE-PROD:4 mounts at most one contextual Trend, Comparison, or Status card inside the Stage host, rendered through the existing evidence visual view. STAGE-PROD:FINAL still defers relationship reveal/hide animation, pointer-depth and richer 3D, and advanced chart or what-if interaction.

SCENE-ORG is the stable host. Center Stage stays primary. Theatre compositions stay exempt from the 0–3 card cap. Detail owns deep data. Saved Scenes store references and view preferences, not object appearance.

A separate orthographic 2D strategic map exists for topology framing. It is a different surface. It is not the isometric object camera.

## Authority the later phases must keep

- Director and Theatre keep scene meaning. OVS consumes visual roles and channels.
- DIR:VI keeps chart semantics. OVS does not create a chart store or recalculate values.
- STAGE-3DOBJ and SP:2 remain the object-appearance authorities until a later phase explicitly consumes them. OVS:1 does not replace them with a parallel form system.
- STAGE-2D:1 remains camera authority. An isometric view is a projection policy on that fixed camera, not orbit and not the 2D map camera.
- SCENE-ORG remains workspace authority. OVS changes what is drawn in Center Stage. It does not move regions, card caps, Detail, Activity, or Saved Scenes.
- Semantic `z = 0` stays the management plane. Visual depth stays appearance. State still must not change form or depth.

## Phase boundaries implied by this audit

OVS:1 defines the isometric object language for existing semantic families, consuming STAGE-3DOBJ and DTH:3 form meaning.

OVS:2 maps certified state, attention, hover, selection, and disclosure onto that language without a second interaction runtime.

OVS:3 maps the nine Nexo roles and DIR:VI evidence visuals into isometric theatre forms, keeping one contextual-visual rule and the NexoTime boundary.

OVS:4 proves the language on the live Executive workspace and certifies it. SCENE-ORG is not redesigned.

## Deferred, not failures

Relationship reveal/hide animation, pointer-depth picking, and advanced what-if interaction remain STAGE-PROD debt. They are inputs to OVS:2 and OVS:3, not open product failures.

OVS:1 was not started.
